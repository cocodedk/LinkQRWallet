Privacy policy - Link QR Wallet

Effective 7 October 2026. This policy describes version 1.0.10 of the app (com.cocode.linkqrwallet).

Summary

Link QR Wallet keeps the links you save on your phone. We run no server for it, and it has no accounts, no analytics and no ads. The app sends us nothing.

The app uses the internet for one thing. When you save a link, the app looks up the page's title in the background, so your list shows a name instead of a web address. Nothing is sent to any website before you save. If you type a title yourself, the app does not contact the website. That website can see the request. The buttons on the About screen, such as “Read the privacy policy”, open a web page in your browser when you tap them. That is your browser connecting, not the app.

What is collected

We collect nothing, because the app has no way to send anything to us. On your phone, the app handles:

  • Links you save. For each link: the address, a title, the domain (the website's name, for example example.com) and the times you added and last changed it.
  • Addresses you type, paste, scan or share into the app. The app keeps one only if you tap “Save”.
  • Camera pictures while the scanner is open. The app reads QR codes (square patterns that can hold a web address) from them. The pictures stay in memory only. The app never saves or sends them.

The app has no account. It never asks for your name, email address or phone number.

Servers the app contacts

Only one kind: the website of a link you save, and any page that website sends the app on to. The app asks that website for the page and reads its title, which is the name your browser shows on the page's tab. The app does this so your list shows names you can recognise. No server of ours is involved. The app follows a redirect (a website sending the app on to another address) only if the new address passes the same checks as the first one (see “Addresses the app refuses” and “Connecting only to checked addresses” below).

  • When it happens. After you tap “Save”, and only if you left the “Title” field empty. The app saves the link first. It then looks up the page's title in the background. One lookup can take more than one request: the web address itself and up to five redirects. Nothing is sent while you type, paste, scan or share an address, or if you cancel. If you type a title yourself, the app does not contact the website at all.
  • What the website sees. An ordinary web request from your phone. It shows the app's name (LinkQRWallet/1.0), your IP address (the number that identifies your internet connection) and the time. If you leave off the start of an address, the app adds https://. If the website sends the request on to another address, the app follows it, up to 5 times, and that address sees the request too. The app connects directly and ignores any proxy set in Android. It follows only an address that passes the checks below, and never follows a redirect from https to http.
  • What the app keeps. The title, and nothing else from the page. The app reads at most the first 256 kilobytes of a page. Of any other answer, such as a redirect or an error, it reads only the headers (the opening lines that give the status and where a redirect leads). The link is already saved by then, with the domain as its title. If the website answers within about 8 seconds, the app replaces the domain with the page's title. That limit applies to each request, so a lookup that follows redirects can take longer. If it does not answer, or the request fails, the domain stays and the app shows no error. If you change the title before the page answers, the app keeps your change.
  • Addresses the app refuses. The app does not save a refused address. It refuses addresses that do not start with http or https; localhost and names ending in .local; names ending in .onion; names starting with xn-- (a coded form of names with special characters); and numbers that stand for your own device, your network or a reserved use. For IPv4 those are 0.0.0.0/8, 10.0.0.0/8, 100.64.0.0/10, 127.0.0.0/8, 169.254.0.0/16, 172.16.0.0/12, 192.0.0.0/24, 192.0.2.0/24, 192.168.0.0/16, 198.18.0.0/15, 198.51.100.0/24, 203.0.113.0/24 and everything from 224.0.0.0 up. For IPv6 they are ::, ::1, fc00::/7, fe80::/10, fec0::/10, ff00::/8, 2001:db8::/32 and 64:ff9b:1::/48 (a range used to translate between IPv4 and IPv6). The number after a slash says how much of the start of an address is fixed, so 10.0.0.0/8 means every address starting with 10. An IPv4 address written inside an IPv6 address is checked as IPv4. An address written as numbers in any way other than four plain decimal parts, such as 127.1, 2130706433, 0177.0.0.1 or 0x7f.0.0.1, is refused, because programs read these differently. An address from a QR code is checked before it reaches the “Add link” screen. Every address is checked again when you tap “Save”, before the app contacts any website.
  • Connecting only to checked addresses. Before it connects, the app asks for the addresses the website's name leads to and checks all of them against the list above. If any one is on the list, the app does not connect. Otherwise it connects only to the addresses it checked, and it checks the address it actually connected to once more before it sends the request. The same happens for every redirect. When a check fails, the link keeps the domain as its title. A link whose name leads to a refused address can still be saved, but the app does not contact it.
  • Buttons that open a web page. The About screen has five: “See the latest version”, “Read the privacy policy”, “Open the website”, “See the source code on GitHub” and “Report a problem on GitHub”. When you tap one, the app hands a fixed address to your browser, and your browser contacts that website itself (github.com or qr.cocode.dk, or f-droid.org once the app is listed there), under that website's own policy. The app itself makes no connection and does not look for updates by itself. “Open link” on a link's details screen works the same way, for the link you saved.

What each website does with the request is up to its own privacy policy. We do not see it.

Permissions

Android asks you to allow some of the things an app wants to use. This app uses two.

  • Camera. If you have not allowed it yet, Android asks when you open the scanner. The app uses the camera only while the scanner is open, to read QR codes. If you say no, you can still add links by typing, pasting or sharing them in.
  • Internet. Used for the title lookup described above, and for nothing else.

The app asks for nothing else: not your contacts, location, microphone, photos or files. Android's support libraries also add one internal permission, DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION, which is private to this app.

What stays on the phone

  • Your links. They are stored in a database (linkqrwallet.db) in the app's private storage on your phone. We do not sync them to any service of ours. To rename a link, open it and change the “Title” field. To delete one, open it, tap “Delete link” and confirm. To remove everything at once, clear the app's data in Android's settings or uninstall the app.
  • QR images you share. When you tap “Share QR code”, the app saves a QR image of the link in its private cache folder (temporary storage that Android can clear). It passes the image and the link's address to Android's share menu, which hands both to the app you pick. The app does not delete these images itself. They stay until Android clears the cache, you clear the app's cache or data, or you uninstall the app.
  • Copy link and Open link. “Copy link” puts a link's address on the clipboard (the phone's temporary store for copied text). The app reads the clipboard only when you choose to paste text into the “Web address (URL)” field yourself. “Open link” passes the address to your browser, which then contacts the website itself, under its own policy.
  • Android backup. If backup is on for your phone, Android can copy app data to your Google account. This app allows that and excludes nothing, so your saved links can be included in your backup with Google and in a phone-to-phone transfer. That is between you and Google, and we have no access to it. You can turn backup off in Android's settings.

Third parties

The app contains no code for analytics, advertising or crash reporting, and no Google Play services or Firebase. Apart from the title lookup, the app's own code makes no network connection. The libraries it is built from (ready-made code from other developers) do these jobs: QR codes (ZXing), the camera (CameraX), the database (Room), the screens (Jetpack Compose), the connection to a website (OkHttp) and the page title (jsoup).

These can see something: the websites you look up (see above), the app you share a QR image with, your browser when you tap “Open link” or a button on the About screen, and Google if you use Android backup.

This website

This website, qr.cocode.dk, is hosted on GitHub Pages. Your browser loads the page's font from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com) and its header and footer from brand.cocode.dk. Each of these servers sees your IP address, as any server would. The site sets no cookies of its own and runs no analytics. This applies to the website only, not to the app.

Your rights

Because the app sends us nothing, we hold no data about you. Your data is on your phone, where you can see, change and delete it as described above. Under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, the EU's data protection law) you also have the right to see your data, to have it corrected or deleted, and to complain. Cocode (Babak Bandpey) is responsible for the app. For what a website you looked up holds, contact that website. You can complain to Datatilsynet, the Danish Data Protection Agency.
